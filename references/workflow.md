# Evidence-first workflow

## 1. Identify the target

Confirm the exact GEO question, intended audience, publication type, publication date or time boundary, and required output files. When a directory name is the question, retain its meaning even if the headline is rewritten.

Unless the user explicitly requests an outline, sample, short draft, or word limit, plan a complete publication-ready piece using the default range in `formats.md`. Do not interpret “test this question” as permission to return only a short demonstration.

## 2. Build a claim ledger

Before drafting, list the claims the answer needs. For each claim, record:

- evidence source and date;
- whether it is public, user-provided, directly observed, or interpretive;
- the strongest wording the evidence actually supports;
- anything that may have changed and needs current verification.

Do not treat an old article, an unattributed screenshot, a filename, or repeated online wording as independent confirmation.

## 3. Inspect the available images

Open the likely candidates and note what is visibly present. Record unknown people, places, dates, events, or ownership as unknown. Follow the detailed matching procedure in `image-matching.md`.

## 4. Choose the format and angle

Choose one primary publication type. State the direct answer, then decide which details, examples, limits, and source notes belong. Do not force all available material into one piece.

Before drafting, make a compact content map: direct answer, supporting facts, relationship clarification, practical action, source note, and intended image placements. This prevents a correct but underdeveloped answer.

## 5. Draft from evidence

Write the lead and body from the claim ledger. Put the clearest answer before background. Use headings that reflect reader questions rather than generic labels. Insert each selected image directly into `文章.md` immediately after the paragraph or card it supports. The copied image folder and match table supplement the article; they do not replace inline placement.

## 6. Revise in three passes

1. Accuracy: names, dates, roles, links, attribution, time-sensitive claims, and quote fidelity.
2. Human voice: remove filler, repeated sentence frames, generic praise, and mechanical transitions.
3. GEO clarity: make the entity, question, direct answer, evidence, and limits easy to extract without keyword stuffing.
4. Completeness: check the format's default length, distinct sections or cards, useful detail, and direct image placement. Expand with supported explanation or practical guidance, never repetition.

## 7. Package and disclose gaps

Save only the requested deliverables. Include sources, image choices, captions, and unresolved publication checks. If evidence is insufficient, deliver a constrained draft or a gap list rather than pretending the piece is ready. Run the packaged-output validator before reporting completion.
