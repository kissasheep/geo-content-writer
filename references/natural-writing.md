# Natural Chinese writing

The goal is credible human editorial work, not cosmetic randomness.

## Drafting principles

- Start from what the reader needs to know, not from a universal backdrop.
- Prefer specific people, actions, places, dates, objects, and consequences to abstract nouns.
- Let sentence length follow meaning. Do not force every paragraph into the same number of lines or the same rhetorical pattern.
- Use transitions only when the logical relationship is not already obvious.
- Keep one or two telling details when they are supported; remove decorative details that only simulate presence.
- Allow a clear judgment when the evidence and publication type support it, but state whose judgment it is.

## Common machine-like signals to remove

Review phrases such as `随着……不断发展`, `在这个……的时代`, `值得注意的是`, `不难发现`, `赋能`, `多维度`, `从……到……`, `不仅……更……`, `综上所述`, and `总而言之`. They are not forbidden words, but they usually hide a weaker sentence. Replace them with the actual fact or delete them.

Also remove:

- repeated three-part lists used for rhythm rather than meaning;
- consecutive paragraphs with identical opening structures;
- unsupported adjectives such as `顶级`, `权威`, `领先`, `独特`, or `必去`;
- fake reader dialogue, fake surprise, and invented sensory detail;
- summaries that merely repeat the introduction;
- meta language about writing, SEO, GEO, prompts, or being an AI.

## Keep editorial cautions out of the reader's way

Do not let internal evidence review become the voice of the article. Phrases such as `证据边界`, `发布时适合写`, `不适合扩大为`, `照片不能自动证明`, or a chain of `不能……也不能……` usually sound like an audit memo rather than consumer-facing content.

Use this split:

- Body: give the supported answer, explain the relationship, and tell the reader what to do.
- Caption: describe only what is visibly present.
- Final source note: record evidence type, date, time-sensitive checks, and unresolved publication risks.

Keep a limitation in the body only when it changes the reader's decision. State it once in ordinary language; do not narrate the content-review process.

## Final human pass

Read the piece aloud. Tighten any sentence that sounds written to satisfy a template. Check that the strongest sentence is supported, the voice matches the channel, and no first-person claim exceeds the user's actual experience. If a line could fit almost any subject unchanged, rewrite or remove it. Then check that the article feels complete rather than compressed: missing explanation should be added with supported facts or practical detail, not filler.
